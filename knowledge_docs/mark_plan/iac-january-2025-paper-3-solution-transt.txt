Part (c) Discuss the key strategic risks and benefits in the decision to
invest in drones that may affect the overall sustainability of SprayT
and provide overall mitigating actions for the strategic risks
identified. Marks
1 Risks that could endanger the viability of the project as a going concern
include sustainability factors as listed below:
2 As the risk of changes to and new developments in technology, leading to
obsolescence, is transferred to TT, this should be included in considerations
for the risk of the project. 1
3 Not going ahead is a threat to going concern as the planes are no longer
competitive with the advantages of drones. 1
4 The actual purchase reveals additional costs such as import duties and
repair costs. A comprehensive consideration of all costs may reveal other
hidden costs. 1
5 Technology is changing swiftly, and the assumption of a useful life of five
years and the disposal values may be inaccurate. 1
6 Public liability due to harming people, causing fires or cyber security threats
could result in large claims. 1
7 The precision and reliability of the drones were tested in a Chinese market,
these may not necessarily be transferable to the African market and/or
environment. 1
8 This will also attract other risks relating to POPIA if the data is shared with
the other farmers. 1
9 Introducing drones might result in plane pilots becoming redundant (should
the pilots not be happy with a lower salary offering and working as drone
pilots) and thus TT may incur high retrenchment costs. 1
10 In hindsight, the market reaction to drones might have been overvalued as
customers only entered in two-year contracts and not the anticipated five
years. 1
11 As the drones need to be stored while not being used and transported to
the particular farm when crop spraying takes place, the risk exists that the
drones could be stolen from storage or while in transit. 1
12 Given that the drones imported from China. TT could be exposed to foreign
exchange risk given the volatility of the South African rand. 1
13 Should any drones under warranty need to be returned to the supplier and
be replaced/repaired, the time taken will disrupt spraying activities. 1
14 The drones could be hacked while in flight and potentially resulting in the
loss of the drone(s). 1
15 Adverse weather conditions could result in the drones not being able to fly,
disrupting the crop spraying process and impact profitability. 1
16 Given that crop spraying, depending on the particular crop, generally does
not take place throughout the year; the drones will not be utilised as
planned, affecting the IRR of the project. 1
Strategic benefits
1 Drones are more environmentally friendly, as they do not use fossil fuel but
rather solar power. With the global trend away from fossil fuels, the drones
are a necessary change. 1
2 Drones use less water, increasingly important with water being the number
one sustainability issue globally currently. 1
3 The financial benefits for farmers in increased yields and lower costs will
make planes particularly less competitive, as it is not only the cost that is
the benefit. 1

4 A decrease in operational costs (such as fewer plane pilots, fuel, water
costs, less waste of pesticides due to increased precision) can be
transferred to customers (in lower pricing) and subsequently make SprayT
more attractive, thus increasing its market share. 1
5 Project Drone increased TT's corporate citizenship flag as it allows it the
opportunity of upskilling local people based on the less stringent licensing
requirements of drone pilots. 1
6 Drones also introduce a potentially new revenue stream for TT. The data
collected by the drones could be shared with the farmers at a fee to assist
them in enhancing their productivity. 1
7 The 20 hours flying ability of the drones allows SprayT to reduce time spent
on each customer and thus able to serve a larger market. 1
Risk mitigations
1 Risk of additional hidden costs – perform more detailed research on all
costs, including import duties and foreign currency exposures. 1
2 Risk changing technology – Perform scenario analyses on the project to
consider shorter lifespan options, and potential earlier replacement options
with different assumptions to create a broader risk profile. 1
3 Public liability risk – Obtain public liability insurance products to address the
losses that would be incurred on any claims. 1
4 Drones not tested in African environment – Obtain a test drone to perform
testing in the South African climate to ensure that drones remain suitable in
the different conditions. 1
5 It is suggested that a forward exchange contract (FEC) could be used to
hedge against the foreign exchange risk at acquisition. 1
6 Cybersecurity measures need to be in place to counteract the risk of
hacking. 1
7 The drones will need to be insured against the risk of theft. 1
