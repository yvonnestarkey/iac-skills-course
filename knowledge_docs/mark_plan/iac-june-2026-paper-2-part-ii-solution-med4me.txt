Marks
1 VAT consequences
1.1 Acquisition of buildings and renovation costs
1.1.1 Input tax may be claimed on the acquisition of the old buildings and
renovation costs because BHealth acquired the goods for the purpose of
making taxable supplies in the course of its enterprise, and the
suppliers levied output tax on the supplies (paragraph (a) of the definition
of "input tax" in section 1(1) of the VAT Act). 1
1.1.2 Input tax on the acquisition may be deducted to the extent that the
buildings will be used to make taxable supplies. As the buildings will
be used 100% for taxable supplies, the full input tax may be claimed
(section 17(1), read with the definition of "input tax" in section 1(1) of the
VAT Act). 1
1.1.3 On the acquisition of fixed property, input tax is only deductible to the
extent that payment has been made. However, as the purchase was
financed by a loan and not by the seller, the full input tax may be claimed
(section 16(3)(a)). 1
1.1.4 Input tax on acquisition of buildings (R2 million + R3,4 million) x 15% =
R810 000 1
1.1.5 Input tax on renovation cost (R3 million + R5 million) x 15% =
R1 200 000 1
1.2 Interest expense
1.2.1 There is no input tax on the interest expense as interest is a financial
service and an exempt supply (no output tax was levied by Med4Me
and RMD Bank) (section 2(1)(f) read with section 12(a) of the VAT Act). 2
1.3 Initiation fees
1.3.1 The initiation fees paid to RMD Bank is deemed not to be a financial
service as the consideration payable is in respect of a fee that relates to
section 2(1)(f) of the VAT Act (proviso to section 2(1) of the VAT Act) and
is therefore a taxable supply. 1
1.3.2 Therefore, BHealth may claim input tax on the initiation fees, as RMD
Bank levied output tax on the supply and the expenditure was incurred in
the course of making taxable supplies. 1

levied by the holding company of the Med4Me on the clinics.

Marks
1 The fact that the amount of the management fee charged, is above a market
related amount, may well mean that the expenditure was not wholly incurred
for purposes of trade, as it may well have been to utilise the assessed
loss. It may therefore be necessary to evaluate whether an impermissible
tax avoidance arrangement exists 1
2 There is an agreement in place as Med4Me charges a management fee
under an agreement 1
3 The agreement entitles Med4Me to management fees, resulting in
income accruing to the company. 1
4 As Med4Me has an assessed loss, it is possible that the intention is to use
the assessed loss to reduce the normal tax liabilities of the other
companies within the same group, effectively shifting profits to the holding
company with no normal tax consequences. (All other group companies
incurred a normal tax liability.) 1
5 As the arrangement results in a tax benefit, the party obtaining that benefit
may be unable to demonstrate that, having regard to the relevant facts and
circumstances, obtaining a tax benefit was not the sole or main purpose
of the avoidance arrangement. 1
6 The agreement may be seen by the Commissioner as an impermissible tax
avoidance arrangement in terms of section 80A of the Income Tax Act, as
the transaction would not have been entered into for bona fide business
purposes other than obtaining a tax benefit. (Presumption of purpose –
section 80G). 1
7 The expenditure, or part thereof, may be disallowed by SARS, and Med4Me
will have to include the full amount of the management fees in its gross
