1 Background
Medical for Me Ltd (‘Med4Me’) is a group of medical companies listed on the Johannesburg
Stock Exchange (JSE). Med4Me was established in the early 2000s, with the purpose of
providing a range of medical, dental, day hospital, mental health and trauma medical services
through its own clinics in larger towns in rural areas. Initially, business was good, and the
company was able to open several clinics in these rural areas.
However, as time went on, management noticed an increasing number of missed
appointments and late arrivals that put pressure on the clinics and medical staff and led to
declining revenues. An investigation revealed that the reason for this decline was that many
of the patients lived far away from the clinics and transport was often unreliable.
As a solution, management decided to ‘decentralise’ its clinics by expanding into smaller towns
by partnering with independent doctors. A newly formed company, BohlokoaHealth Ltd
(‘BHealth’) was founded and incorporated in South Africa for this purpose. Med4Me and the
independent doctors (all natural persons) own 52% and 48% respectively of the issued share
capital of BHealth. Accordingly, Med4Me exercises control over BHealth, which is consolidated
in the Med4Me group accounts. The financial year end of all companies in the Med4Me Group
is 31 December. All companies in the group apply IFRS® Accounting Standards, are South
African residents for income tax purposes and registered vendors in terms of the VAT Act.

2 Patient loyalty programme
Med4Me was searching for an innovative way to attract and keep its patients. Med4Me’s
market research has shown that patients visit a clinic only when they have a health
issue. However, Med4Me wished to change that by promoting a healthy lifestyle by
motivating and encouraging patients to come to its clinics for regular checkups. It
therefore launched a patient loyalty programme during FY2025 specifically for BHealth
clinics. BHealth soon noticed a shift in patient behaviour and also found that the loyalty
programme was attracting new patients to the clinics. An additional benefit is that it
enabled BHealth to gather valuable health information about its patients that it could use
to provide a higher quality of service.
Patients earn points, called HealthMulas, based on their spend on a defined list of
preventative health check services at BHealth clinics, such as glucose tests and blood
pressure tests. One HealthMula is earned for every R1 spent at a clinic and each
HealthMula can be redeemed for R1. So, for example, a blood pressure test that costs
R50 will earn 50 HealthMulas, and a glucose test that costs R80 will earn 80
HealthMulas. In this way, patients build up a HealthMula savings account. The
HealthMulas can be used as consideration to pay for products and medicine in the
pharmacy and future treatments at the clinic. The HealthMulas cannot be used to pay
for the preventative health checks on the defined list and cannot be exchanged for cash.

3 Share appreciation rights (SARs)
On 1 January 2024, Med4Me, the holding company, granted 200 SARs each to 16
BHealth executive directors. The SARs entitle the executive directors to a cash payment
if the Med4Me share price appreciates over time, to be paid by Med4Me. BHealth has
no obligations to settle the scheme. The vesting of the SARs is subject to vesting
conditions and the vesting date is 31 December 2025.
The vesting conditions are as follows:
• Remaining in the employ of BHealth until 31 December 2025.
• Profit after tax per the management accounts for BHealth in those two years
should be at least R10 million cumulatively.
• The number of SARs will be doubled at vesting date if the profit after tax per the
management accounts for BHealth is at least R15 million cumulatively in those two
years.
• The final SARs cash payout value is based on the Med4Me closing price on the
JSE on 30 December 2025.
By 31 December 2024, only one executive director had left BHealth’s employ and
BHealth estimated that a further two executive directors would leave by 31 December
2025. However, only one executive director left BHealth during FY2025.

4 Lease agreement
On 1 January 2025, Med4Me entered into a lease agreement to obtain a medical waste
and sterilisation machine, for a non-cancellable lease period of five years, with Sango
Medical Equipment, a third party. The first lease payment was R285 000 per annum
payable in arrears on 31 December 2025. The agreement stipulates an annual increase
on 31 December that is based on the published consumer price index (CPI) rate during
the preceding 12 months. The published CPI was 5,2% on 1 January 2025 and 6,5% on
31 December 2025. The first increase in lease payments will take place on 31 December
2026 and annually thereafter.
Med4Me does not have an option to purchase the medical waste and sterilisation
machine at the end of the lease term, but the lease agreement provides for an option to
extend the lease for a further three years. At lease inception and to date, Med4Me had
the intention to extend the lease.
The medical waste and sterilisation machine has the latest technology and is regularly
updated by Sango Medical Equipment. Med4Me incurred installation costs of R167 000
on 1 January 2025, which were only payable six months later on 30 June 2025.
Med4Me’s incremental borrowing rate was 9% per annum (compounded annually) on
1 January 2025. The interest rate implicit in the lease was not readily determinable by
Med4Me.
The group presents right-of-use lease assets as part of property, plant and equipment
in the SoFP and lease liabilities as part of other non-current liabilities and other current
liabilities respectively in the SoFP.
