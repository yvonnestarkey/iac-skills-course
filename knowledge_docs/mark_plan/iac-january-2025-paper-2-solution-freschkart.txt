Part (a) Discuss the key risks that FreschKart was exposed to in its
operating environment before the company shifted to an eco-
friendly strategy.
• Ignore risks that would be addressed by a shift to an eco-
friendly strategy.
Marks
1 Concentration / Market risk
1.1 The highly competitive nature of the South African retail industry could lead
to challenges in attracting and retaining customers.
1
1.2 Price conscious consumers will make it difficult for FreschKart to increase its
prices to help the company to turn a profit.
1
1.3 There is an over reliance on the SA leg of the business (which accounts for
>90% of all revenue)
1
2 Exchange rate risk
2.1 FreschKart operates in several countries around Africa and the company
could be negatively affected by fluctuations (rand appreciation) in the local
currency.
1
2.2 Furthermore, FreschKart imports some of its products from other countries,
thus the company could be forced to pay higher prices for imports should the
rand depreciate.
1
3 Financial and operational risk
3.1 The volatility of interest rates in the countries in which FreschKart operates
increases the risks of the company being unable to repay its debts, as debt
costs are likely to rise.
1
3.2 FreschKart’s high gearing level will affect the company’s ability to raise new
debt finance and how much debt it can support (debt capacity). Given its high
gearing level, FreschKart may find that it cannot raise any more debt finance,
or it may find that new debt will be at a significantly higher interest rate shown
by the proposed Bank Finance Ltd loan.
2
3.3 High gearing leads to a high interest obligation for FreschKart and an
increased risk of being unable to pay all its interest given the company’s
reduction in profits and low cash flow.
1
3.4 Escalating debt may cause the company to further be in breach of its key
covenant ratios (debt/equity) which could result in further restrictions on
leading for the company or increases in debt repayment costs.
1
3.5 Increased volatility of earnings and the declining share price will increase the
cost of equity, making equity finance more expensive. As seen by the
financial risk premium when comparing the asset beta of 0,48 with the equity
beta of 4,84.
2
3.6 Risks of strikes in the industry may have a negative effect on the company’s
ability to operate efficiently, which will lead to a loss of customers that favour
efficiency.
1
3.7 FreschKart’s decision not to increase its managers salaries may impact the
company’s ability to retain good management, which may have a negative
effect on the company’s ability to improve its performance.
1
3.8 FreschKart uses its own drivers to deliver goods ordered via the app - they
may therefore be held liable if their drivers cause accidents or are themselves
injured on the road (or risks around hiring and vetting of drivers process).
1
4 Liquidity risk
4.1 FreschKart’s lack of cash flow increases the risk that the company would be
unable to repay its creditors on time, resulting in default on supplier terms
and possible restrictions on supplier sales to the company.
1
5 Technology risk

5.1 As the company’s GoFresch application is dependent on the functionality of
the operating system, there is a risk that the system may be hacked and stop
operations from continuing.

5.2 The GoFresch application requires constant upgrades due to the rapid pace

Part (b) Critically evaluate the strategic actions that FreschKart has
undertaken to become more eco-friendly. Marks
1 Alignment with values
• 1.1 The suggested strategy shows a commitment by FreschKart to sustainability,
as the company is taking a proactive step to improve its environmental
influence by reducing the company’s reliance on non-ecofriendly electricity
generation sources and focusing on sustainable water harvesting techniques.
1
• 1.2 The removal of plastic bags for holding of customer goods is also a good way
to project the company as being a sustainable retailer, given the effects of
plastic on the environment.
1
• 1.3 The rebranding does well to cover most of the operations of the company as
no more non-environmentally safe products will be sold by FreschKart stores.
However, no mention is made of how non-environmentally safe products,
which may still be sold at the company’s CB stores, will be treated. Will these
products still continue to be sold or will they form part of the resale to
agreement with Pick and Choose? 1
• 1.4 By selling the non-environmentally safe products to another retailer, there is
a likelihood that such products will still be sold to the public and will therefore
still possibly cause harm to the environment through production or disposal.
1
• 1.5 FreschKart could also be said to be assisting in increasing the demand for
such products as these products will now be sold at cheaper prices by the
Pick and Choose, which could encourage more customers to purchase them.
Thus this would not seem to align to the company values.
1
• 1.6 The published list of environmental commitments is good; however, the
company does not seem to want to report selectively on its achievements in
this area, which shows a lack of transparency to the public about the
company’s commitment to real environmental change as there is no
accountability for non-achievements in this area.
1
• 1.7 The marketing of the company as an environmentally friendly company could
be seen as the company ‘greenwashing’ its business given the above. This

To: Board of Directors

Possible Rights Issue
1 Introduction
• 1.1 I would agree that issuing rights would be good initiative to recapitalise the
company and improve the company’s current debt servicing costs.
1
2 Effect on share price
• 2.1 The rights issue may have an initial positive effect on the share price of
FreschKart as shareholders would likely be encouraged by the company’s
decision to reduce its financial leverage.
1
• 2.2 It is true however that the rights issue would reduce the company’s share price
to below the board’s targeted levels of between R20 and R35 for the
company’s share price. This would result in a loss of shareholder value.
1
• 2.3 The current estimated ex-rights price for the shares is R19,68, which is below
the targeted range. However market sentiment to the rights issue may
increase the share price if investors believe that the rights issue is warranted.
1
• 2.4 The negative growth shown by the company over time (-13%) may increase
the chances of investors not being interested in the rights issue.
1
• 2.5 However, the rights issue would give shareholders the opportunity to sell their
rights, which may allow the company to gain a new type of investor who is
willing take more financial risk.
1
• 2.6 The lack of a successful history with rights issues increases the risk that the
current rights issue may not be successful, which would increase the chances
of a further decline in share price.
1
• 2.7 Delaying the rights issue may continue to have a negative impact on the
company’s share price as the market is clearly not encouraged by the
increasing debt (as evidenced by the difference in asset and equity beta).
1
3 Key ratios
• 3.1 The rights issue would have a positive effect on key debt and financial ratios,
which may help to encourage shareholders about the prospects of the
company. .
1
• 3.2 The issuing of the rights would also have a major effect on the current
solvency of the company, as the company’s solvency ratio would improve by
3%.
1
• 3.3 Thus, this would still be above the company’s targeted debt to total firm value
and above some of the entities covenant ratios, which means that the
company would still need to raise more equity after the issue. This may further
negatively affect the share price.
1
• 3.4 The interest cover ratio would improve slightly, which would help to reduce the
overall company risk. In addition, interest costs would reduce, which would
improve overall cash flow of the company.
1
• 3.5 Not having the rights issue now would continue to negatively affect the
company’s ability to raise debt increase the cost of debt and further erode
profits.
1
• 3.6 A non-rights issue would make it harder for the company to pay dividends, as
it may struggle in future to meet solvency ratios as required by the Companies
Act. This may further discourage potential investment.
1

Part (d) Discuss the ethical behaviour and ethical leadership of FreschKart
as demonstrated by the directors towards the following
stakeholders: the lenders, suppliers represented by Cisty, the
store managers and the local communities.
Marks
1 Ethical behaviour refers to doing what is good for self and also for others,
even when no one sees it.
1
2 Directors are expected to display personal ethics in their capacity, business
ethics as representative of FreschKart (tone from the top) and display
professional ethics as presumably some of them are members of
professional bodies.
That is, they need to act with integrity and honesty, be straightforward and
with fairness and uphold stakeholder inclusivity.
For a company that adopts King IV, principle 16 requires that the directors
uphold stakeholder inclusiveness.
Moreover, ethical theories require that the company upholds laws and
regulations, which include company policies.
1
1

3 Refinancing existing loans at favourable terms, is good for FreschKart from
a cash flow commitment perspective and demonstrates responsibility to
existing lenders, safeguarding any defaults, and upholding and honouring of
commitments.
1
4 Due to FreschKart’s pursuit of an eco-friendly strategy, withdrawing from the
Cisty contract was good for FreschKart. Moreover, FreschKart’s
communication with Cisty, and honouring of its commitment, being the lower
of continuing with the contract or incurring the penalty, is a responsible and
ethical act towards Cisty.
1
5 Revising the leave day policy from vesting to non-vesting, as an example,
in response to FreschKart’s poor financial results, and negotiating with store
managers on no salary increases and employer contributions are good for
FreschKart that is struggling financially, but also retaining all store managers
and not retrenching them especially under the prevailing circumstances is
in the interest of the store managers.
However, the zero increase to salaries may create unhappiness, lead to
labour union involvement, potential unrest, a desire to change jobs by store
managers, reduce morale, etc.

and business standing on sustainability and compliance with King IV.
It is equally beneficial to the majority of FreschKart’s customers, that is,
communities in which FreschKart operates. It ensures their sustainability
and their safety.
This is consistent with the consequential point of view theory or good for self
and others, where an ethical choice should be considered from benefitting
the majority.
1
1

communicating changes and responding to prevailing challenges, means
that FreschKart, represented by its directors, is acting ethically and is
displaying ethical leadership. They consider the interests of other
