1 Financial risks
1.1 As a non-profit organization (NPO), Beita was involved in the
community development in Coega and received benefits because it
focused on upskilling the community. With the dissolution of the NPO,
Beita Furniture may not receive the financial incentives as it may no
longer meet the requirements.
1.5
1.2 Furthermore, the risk by transferring assets from a PBO to a private
company, this could –
• jeopardise its tax-exempt status,
• which would trigger capital gains tax, donations tax, or transfer duty
if not done at fair market value.
1.5
1.5
1.3 As a for-profit company, Beita runs the risk of a reduction in donations to fund
the operations.
The risk of Beita could face liquidity and solvency problems if the future
funding of the company is not appropriately structured.
1.5
1.5
1.3.1 If it does receive donations because its profit company has the same
name as the NPO, it runs the risk that it will be expected to pay back
the donated funds if it is not able to provide the donors with a s18A
certificate.
1.5
1.3.2 A reputational risk may arise from this financial risk if the donors get
the impression that Beita is misleading donors into thinking it is still an
NPO.
Allegations of misrepresentation may lead to legal or regulatory action,
which could result in investigations, penalties, fines imposed on Beita.
1.5
1.5
1.3.3 The risk that the donors may also change the terms and conditions
relating to donations already made to the for-profit company., which
Beita may not be in a position to meet, resulting in liquidity issues
should the donations need to be repaid or more onerous conditions
being applied.
1.5
1.4 Depending on the value paid by Dino and Siphiwe for the shares taken
up (scenario is silent in terms of the value paid), there could be non-
compliance with the Companies Act, which could result in potential
fines etc.
1.5

1.6 Employee payments:
1.6.1 Beita is exposed to the risk of insufficient profits being ploughed back
into the company given the payment of 50% of its profits to staff in its
employee for at least six months, which will hamper future growth
prospects of the company.
1.5
1.6.2 There is a risk that insufficient furniture production and sales may
render the business model financially unsustainable, while employees
continue to be paid below the statutory minimum wage.
In addition, there is a risk of exposing Beita to labour law non-
compliance, financial penalties, legal claims, and reputational damage.
1.5
1.5
1.6.3 The profit payouts are made to employees who have been with the
company for more than six months, which will mean that employees
who have been with the company for less than six months will still earn
below the minimum wage.
In addition, there is a risk that this could lead to union action, unhappy
and unproductive employees, fines, reputational damage, etc., in other
words both financial as well as operational risk.
1.5
1.5
1.6.4 Incentives provided to workers may drive the wrong behaviour,
especially when the focus is on short-term profit.
1.5
1.7 Dino and Siphiwe decided to pay a dividend during FY2025:
1.7.1 There is no indication that they considered the liquidity of the company
as required by the Companies Act and if no such considerations were
made, company may in future be unable to meet its short-term
obligations.
1.5
1.8 AsBeitaisnolongeraPBO,itmaynotbe
possible for it to continue to purchase the pallets at a reduced price of
R10 per pallet.
1.5
1.8.1 This may result in a significant increase in the cost of raw materials if
market-related prices are paid, which it may not be able to recover in
its selling price.
1.5
1.9 AmonthlysalaryofR15000wasofferedto
Joseph and Fulani until the sale of the furniture could cover Beita’s
monthly costs:
1.9.1 By merely ‘covering the monthly costs’ with the sales, breakeven is
indicated. If both employees receive an increase when Beita is
breaking even, it will lead to the company making a loss once again.
The timing of the increase is uncertain.
1.5
1.10.1 Existing operations: No indication is given in the scenario if the rental
cost of the factory, (which was not market-related), will increase to a
market-related cost, or what the timing of such a change will be. This
again will increase the cost base of the business and may not be
recoverable in the price of the products.
1.5
1.5
1.10.2 Future operations: The current floor space could limit Beita’s ability to
grow in future, having to rent more expensive property will increase
the fixed costs of Beita, potentially resulting in losses and impacting
negatively on cash flow.
1.5
1.5

1.11.1 Own assets: Beita not having insured its assets can result in financial
losses in the event of theft of, or damage to the assets.
1.5
1.11.2 Absence of third-party insurance would be a risk should Joseph need
to borrow a vehicle in future, and he is involved in an accident.
1.5
1.12 Beita’s failure to invest in new equipment could result in ever-
increasing maintenance costs, which could reduce Beita’s profit as
well as its future cash flows.
1.5
1.13 There is a risk that Beita's products are purchased on an infrequent
basis because of the nature of the tables/benches, limiting
opportunities for repeat business from the Swallows Group: negatively
affecting future sales and cash flow
1.5
1.5
1.14 The company does not have proper financial governance structures in
place, such proper vetting of suppliers could lead to the purchasing of
inferior quality materials, or overpaying for materials purchased, or not
receiving the pallets purchased, resulting in financial losses.
1.5
1.5
1.15 Should Beita contravene the development criteria of the Coega
Development Corporation (CDC) then Beita will lose out on the
Customs Duty and employment tax incentives, negatively impacting
the financial sustainability of Beita.
1.5
2 Operational risks
2.1 Should the quality of Beita’s output not improve going forward, there is
a risk that Beita’s sales would be negatively impacted, as they are no
longer a PBO.
1.5
2.2 Should the new wood treatment process not be tested before being
implemented, there is a risk of inferior quality output being produced,
impacting Beita’s sales negatively.
1.5
2.3 Inferior quality output would likely expose Beita to reputational risk, which too
would negatively impact Beita’s profitability and value.
1.5
2.4 The company may incur warranty or damage claims if poor quality furniture
fails or is returned by customers
1.5
2.5 There is a risk that the company procures stolen goods or becomes
involved in corrupt activities, resulting in legal, financial, regulatory,
and reputational risks.
1.5
2.6 The company may be in breach of laws and regulations such as the
Companies Act if their auditors perform management functions.
1.5
2.7 It is likely that as Beita’s competitors are well-established, there is a
risk that they will not gain a big enough share of the market.
1.5
2.8 Should market research around customer preferences not be carried
out, there is a risk that Beita will not meet customer expectations
resulting in low demand, lost revenue, and inefficient use of resources.
1.5
1.5
2.9 Beita is exposed to the risk of trained staff not being retained resulting
in a loss of skills and institutional knowledge, and workforce instability
resulting in a disruption in its operations.
1.5
2.10 There is a risk that the transition to a for-profit model (change in
strategic intent) has diminished Beita’s contribution to community
development, resulting in reduced social impact.
1.5
1.5

2.11 No evidence of insurance being in place:
From an operational perspective, there is a risk that inadequate
insurance coverage for factory-related accidents could expose Beita to
substantial financial, legal, and operational consequences arising from
employee injuries, property damage, or other incidents impacting
business continuity
1.5
1.5
2.12 Joseph had to borrow a vehicle to pick up pallets. The fact that there is
no delivery vehicle, creates a distribution risk for the company.
1.5
2.13 Dino and Siphiwe studied interior design and do not have any
managerial or financial management experience. This could lead to
poor management of the company.
1.5
2.14 The equipment purchased was purchased second hand, which could
lead to breakdowns that could in turn result in down-time that will
disrupt production.
1.5
2,15 Beita's reliance on factory space that it does not own, combined with
its limited trainee workforce, may constrain its production capacity and
ability to scale operations, resulting in an inability to meet increased
demand from new boutique hotel contracts, potential loss of revenue,
customer dissatisfaction, and reputational damage.
1.5
1.5
2.16 Zolani lacks experience:
2.16.1 There appears to be limited review of Zolani’s work, resulting in her
potentially issuing erroneous reports, which could lead to poor
decision-making.
1.5
2.16.2 The lack of a dedicated financial management function, coupled with
Zolani’s split focus between Finance and HR, increases the risk of
weak financial governance, inadequate controls, and ineffective
oversight of the organisation’s financial resources.
1.5
2.17 The lack of segregation of duties at director level increases the risk of
management override of controls, resulting in unauthorised
transactions, fraud, financial misstatements, and ineffective
governance going undetected.
1.5
2.18 Beita has a dependency risk on Joseph and Fulani; should they
resign, it is likely that it will be difficult to replace them, which will
result in Beita's operations being disrupted.
