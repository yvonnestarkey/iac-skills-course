Part (e) Discuss the business ethics and professional ethical considerations
that NKT would need to consider about the alleged impact of defaults
on loan covenants.
• Include relevant safeguards.
• Assume that NKT also performed the interim review for the year.
Marks
1 ISSUE 1: Pressure to conceal breach of loan covenants to avoid
impact
1.1. Business ethics considerations (including King IV Principle 1 & 3)
from management’s perspective
1.1.1. Inpahla management might be under pressure to conceal the breach in
loan covenants (to avoid its impact), which would compromise their
ethical leadership due to failure to act transparently (King IV. P1.RP1f).
1
1.1.2. Management might engage in dubious or fraudulent actions to conceal
material misstatements and avoid a modified audit opinion.
1
1.1.3. This would lead to a lack of fairness towards the public and government
(key stakeholders of the company) (King IV.P1.RP1e), which would lead to
loss of public trust.
1
1.1.4. Due to the impact that default on loan covenants could have on the
company and potentially the country, management is incentivised to
manipulate the financial statements to hide high debt exposure.
Engaging in such an action would lead to a lack of integrity (due to
dishonesty), hence failing to lead ethically as required by King IV
(P1.RP1a).
1
1
1.2. Professional ethics considerations for NKT arising from management
pressure to conceal breach of loan covenants to avoid impact
1.2.1. The auditors might fail to identify and report on reportable
irregularities and material irregularities, hence failing to comply with the
Auditing Professions Act.
1
1.2.2. The auditors might not act appropriately in accordance with the
requirements of the SAICA/IRBA Code of Professional Conduct and
NOCLAR requirements if they come across suspected or actual non-
compliance with key financial reporting legislation in the SOC.
1
2. ISSUE 2: Management may attempt to unduly influence, through
intimidation/emotional blackmail, the auditors' audit opinion
2.1. Business ethics considerations (including King IV Principle 1 & 3)
from management’s perspective

auditor’s sound judgement using emotional blackmail of the alleged
potential impact this could have on the country to avoid a modified audit
opinion.
1

2.2. Ethical theories consideration
2.2.1. Giving in to pressure to conceal a breach of loan covenants would exhibit

its stakeholders (the government and the public).

2.3. Companies Act of 2008 (as amended) fiduciary duty considerations:
2.3.1. If the directors engage in actions to manipulate the financial statements

dishonest) and the directors would be in breach of their fiduciary duty in
terms of the Companies Act (s76).

Part (h) Formulate the audit procedures to perform at the FY2026 year end
to audit the going concern of Inpahla.
Marks
1 Standard procedures to assess the going concern status of Inpahla
1.1 Obtain a management representation letter dealing specifically with the
going concern of Inpahla and the status of the turnaround strategies
proposed by management.
1
1.2 Inspect the board meetings and have discussions with management on the
going concern status of the company and assess whether
management’s plans (including the turnaround strategies) are significant
to overcome the adverse effects of the operational challenges and high
debt levels.
1
1.3 Obtain the cash flow budgets and forecast from Impala and perform
the following procedures:
1.3.1 Recalculate/cast and cross-cast the forecasts to verify the accuracy of
the forecast.
1
1.3.2 Determine whether the period used by management is appropriate and for
at least 12 months.
1
1.3.3 Inspect the cash flow forecasts for obvious errors and omissions (for
example, inclusion of non-cash flow terms, duplicate amounts, direction of
the in and outflows)
1
1.3.4 Compare the cash flow forecast for the current financial year with events
after the financial reporting date (subsequent to year-end) to identify
whether adjustments are required to the cash flow forecast/whether
additional information is available about amounts included in the forecast.
1
1.3.5 Obtain the budgets and forecast for 2025 and compare with the actual
results as per the trial balance/management accounts for 2025 and
consider the reliability and accuracy of management’s budgeting and
forecasts (based on experience, the competence of management in terms
of preparing forecasts, etc.);
1
1.3.6 Through discussion with management (or inspection of supporting
documentation), assess the knowledge, experience, and competence
of the individual(s) who prepared the cash flow forecasts.
1
1.3.7 Through inspection of cash flow forecast/tmanagement discussions,
assess whether there are indicators of management bias in selecting the
method, significant assumptions and data used in performing the cash flow
forecast (ISA 540).
1
1.3.8 Through discussion with management, assess whether management has
taken the appropriate steps to understand and address estimation
uncertainty (ISA 540).
1
1.3.9 Where management has not taken the appropriate steps to understand
and address estimation uncertainty, request that they perform
additional procedures to do so (ISA 540).

3.1 Enquire from the government, as a shareholder (minister), the status of
