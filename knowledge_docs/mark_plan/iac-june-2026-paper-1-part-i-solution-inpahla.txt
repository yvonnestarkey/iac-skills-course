the current situation of Inpahla.
Marks
Proposal 1: Government funding and an increase in debt
Part I © SAICA 20264

PAPER 1 PART I
1.1 The current situation of the company is dire, with declining profitability, a
deterioration of revenues, a poor debt position with a high debt-to-equity
ratio, and a declining cash interest cover ratio (from 4 times to 1,93 times).
Therefore, any turnaround strategy should improve the company's
long-term financial sustainability without further compromising its
financial position.
1
1.2 In addition to the financial state of the company, the government as the
sole shareholder is also in a problematic state with reducing tax revenues
and increasing debt. The ability of government to provide further equity
would be in question (i.e. it may need to raise tax revenue or divert
resources from other departments/functions).
1
1.3 Proposal one requires a guarantee from a shareholder who is in a difficult
financial space itself, which is unlikely to be attractive to the providers of
debt capital.
1
1.4 In addition, the guarantee will be used to raise further debt in Inpahla,
which would exacerbate the debt and interest positions, and potentially
place the company in breach of covenants.
1
1.5 The existing debt covenants would potentially prohibit this action, and
the company may need to engage with debt providers to relax their
covenants in order to follow this course of action, which would be a difficult
sell.
1
1.6 The cost of the additional debt financing may be significantly higher than
anticipated given Inpahla's weakened financial position and increased credit
risk, reducing the attractiveness of Proposal 1
1
1.7 The concerns regarding the deteriorating domestic inflation outlook could
lead to the SA Reserve Bank raising interest rates to counteract
inflationary increases. This would lead to increased interest payments for
Inpahla, as well as reduced profit and cash, which would further harm its
ability to service debt payments when due.
1
1.8 The proposed funding option should be aligned with Inpahla's historic
strategic objectives and capital structure strategy which seemed
adequate, rather than addressing only the immediate funding shortfall.
1
1.9 Although the government may not receive an attractive financial return on
the additional equity investment, it may still consider the investment
appropriate given its broader service delivery and socio-economic
objectives.
1
1.10 Additional government funding may be politically difficult to justify, given
the historical performance of state-owned entities and increasing public
scrutiny over the use of public resources.
1
1.11 The relaxing of regulations for procurement could have a significant
impact on the company costs and revenues, with no need for further
investment. It should be pursued irrespective of the funding option chosen.
1
1.12 Relaxing the procurement requirements may improve operational efficiency;
however, it could also weaken governance controls and increase the risk
of fraud, corruption and procurement irregularities

Proposal 2: Privatisation
2.1 The second proposal to privatise assets is a good option as it would not
lead to a deterioration in the financial position of either the government
or the company.
1
2.2 Government will likely need to share control of Inpahla, and decision
making will need to incorporate the preferences of the private investors that
might lead to conflict of interest for Inpahla
1
2.3 The privatisation option could also bring in new capital and skills which
could provide critical assistance with turning the company around and
improving the financial performance and position.
1
2.4 Private investors are likely to prioritise profitability over broader social
and policy objectives. Consequently, the government may need to
compromise some of these objectives to attract private sector participation.
1
2.5 The partnership with private sector participants with other assets might
unlock opportunities and synergistic benefits for Inpahla.
1
2.6 The government would not need to inject additional capital, but could have
the benefit of increased tax collections from the partnership profits and
the clients’ improved profits, as well as from a general increased tax
collection from the broader economic impact it would have in all tax areas.
1
2.7 Private sector participation may improve stakeholder confidence through
stronger commercial expertise, improved governance, greater operational
discipline and enhanced accountability.
1
2.8 Privatisation of state-owned assets is often politically sensitive.
Government, political parties and the public may oppose transferring
part ownership of strategic national infrastructure to private investors,
particularly where transport infrastructure is regarded as a national asset.
Such opposition could delay or prevent implementation. Social goals might
be compromised in favour of profit.
1
2.9 Inpahla has significant going concern issues and questions surrounding the
recent management of the company which would bring into question
whether companies would be interested in partnering with them.
1
2.10 If assets are transferred into subsidiaries, tax implications need to be
considered - recoupments and CGT might arise.
