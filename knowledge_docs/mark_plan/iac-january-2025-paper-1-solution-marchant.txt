Part (b) Discuss, from an income tax and VAT perspective, whether it would
have been more advantageous for VendiBoys to acquire the shares in
OMV, or to have purchased the assets of the business.
• Calculations are not required.
Marks
INCOME TAX
1 Shares
1.1 If VendiBoys buys the shares, the interest payable will not be incurred in
the production of its income of VendiBoys derived from carrying on any
trade. 1
1.2 As the dividends are exempt from normal tax, the deduction of the
interest is prohibited (section 23(f). 1
1.3 and it is not for purpose of trade, as the investment in shares is held for
investment purposes; 1
1.4 However, s24O could be applicable;
1.5 VendiBoys acquires at least 70% (in fact, 100%) of the shares (VendiBoys
and VendiMan will form part of the same group of companies after the
acquisition); 1
1.6 VendiMan is an operating company as defined; at least 80% of its income
comes from selling goods and/or rendering of service. That is, 100% of its
income is from selling goods; and 1
1.7 VendiMan's business is carried on continuously; 1
1.8 Thus, the interest, as defined in section 24J calculated using the yield to
maturity and on a day-to-day basis, will be deductible under section 24O. 1
1.9 VendiBoys will buy the ‘history’ of VendiMan, which may include
unknown liabilities, risks and claims against purchaser. This will include any
potential tax claims for inappropriate deductions made or under declaration of
income. 1
1.10 The shares will have a base cost of R692 925. 1
2 Assets
2.1 In terms of section 24J, the finance costs are incurred in the production of
income, and VendiBoys acquired the assets to produce income; and 1
2.2 for purposes of trade, VendiBoys is trading.

using the yield to maturity and on a day-to-day basis. 1
2.4 Buying assets allows a step-up in the base cost for capital allowances

3 VAT
(The transfer of the shares would be subject to the Securities Transfer Tax.)
3.1 The acquisition, or disposal of the shares, would have no value-added tax

Part (e) Discuss Vendiboys’ business and financial risks arising from the
purchase and operation of the assets from OMV.
Marks
1 The acquisition of assets from Jackie meant that Marchant had to scale up his
operations and incur more costs.
1
2 As it is now a significantly larger business, there is a greater risk that sales could
be insufficient to cover fixed costs (mostly employee costs and depreciation) which
could require additional funding. 1
3 Most of the additional costs are fixed. This increases the risk that the business will
be making a loss if sales are not sufficient to cover the fixed costs.
1
4 Both businesses are in a loss-making positions, although the losses are distorted
by the inclusion of personal expenses in the results.
1
5 Unless sales increase or costs are cut, this would not be a sustainable business. 1
6 Marchant has a good knowledge of student snack preferences. He does not have
this knowledge of preferences for a broader range of corporate customers, served
by the additional vending machines.
If his product mix or pricing is incorrect because of this lack of understanding
of his customers, his sales volumes could decline.
1
1
7 There is a risk that incorrect product choices are being made, causing an increase
in slow-moving stock. This will result in stock write-offs, selling prices that need to
be reduced for slow-moving stock or that the products sold are not as profitable as
those when Marchant started his business.
1
1
8 There is a risk that the machines are placed in areas that do not attract high traffic
or in locations that are close to retailers who offer the same products at lower prices.
1
9 After Covid many call centres and other business implemented hybrid working
arrangements, resulting in fewer employees being at the office, which will
negatively affect sales.
1
10 There is no insurance for theft or damage to the vending machines or inventory,
which means that VendiBoys is exposed to loss of revenue if the machines are out
of order or machines or inventory are stolen.
1
11 Marchant's brother has a lot of responsibility in the operations of VendiBoys. He
manages staff, oversees deliveries and monitors inventory levels. Yet, he has no
experience in supply chain management. There is a risk that, due to
mismanagement, machines are not properly refilled and sales are lost due to
shortages in machines.
1
1
12 The entire purchase consideration was funded by debt, exposing the company to
solvency and liquidity risks.
1
13 It is not clear whether the loans are secured or whether the lenders have any
recourse to the assets of VendiBoys.
1
14 Both loans are repayable at the same time, exposing VendiBoys to refinancing risk.
Both loans are repayable in three years’ time which means that the additional
vending machines must provide excess returns within that time frame. This
increases the liquidity risk for VendiBoys.
In view of the age of some of the machines, there is a risk that some of them may
need to be decommissioned within the next three years, which is the repayment
period of the loan. This will create cash flow constraints in VendiBoys.
1
1
1
15 Since Jack purchased the machines over many years, they are likely to be in a less
suitable condition and may need to be repaired or upgraded. There is a risk that
increased or unexpected maintenance may be required as a result of the age of the
older machines.

Part (f) Identify and discuss, with reference to VendiBoys, the risks of material
misstatement at the assertion level for FY2024 for the following
account balances:
• Inventory: existence, completeness and valuation; and
• Property, plant and equipment: valuation.
Marks
1 Inventory: existence, completeness and valuation
1.1 The financial statements will be used by banks for future funds. There is,
therefore, a risk that management will overstate inventory to ensure that the
financial results are favourable. Therefore, an incentive exists to overstate
assets, specifically inventory (existence).
1
1.2 The occasional connectivity issues experienced with the OpKonnect platform
could result in inaccurate reported inventory figures (all assertions).
1
1.3 The information from the app is not integrated with a financial accounting
system; it is done ‘manually’ by Marchant when compiling the AFS, which
increases the risk that the inventory value figures are inaccurate (all
assertions).
1
1.4 The vending machines are in public areas, which increases the risk that
inventory could be stolen (theft), and there is a risk that inventory at year end
does not exist (existence).
1
1.5 The theft from machines, especially at university campuses, increased the risk
of inventory being damaged (valuation) or stolen (existence).
1
1.6 Some of the vending machines stock products that have a limited ‘shelf life’, ,
and the risk exists at year end that inventory has expired and that the value
should be written down (valuation).
1
1.7 The panel van is used by the technicians and can carry many inventory
items (to optimise route and replenishment):
1

consider the good for self and good for others in making ethical decisions.
