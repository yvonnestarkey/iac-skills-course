Part (e) Based on IFRS® Accounting Standards, evaluate, with reasons,
Sarah’s statement regarding the treatment of pledged inventory and
the loan from XYZ bank.
Marks
Inventory considerations
MT will control the inventory items if it has the ability to direct the use of and
obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from the inventory items
which includes having the ability to prevent other entities from directing the use of
and obtaining the benefits from the inventory.
1
MT has the ability to direct the use of the inventory items because MT can
decide how to use the inventory items and to sell or not to sell the inventory
items.
1
MT has the ability to prevent other entities to obtain substantially all of the
remaining benefits (i.e. rewards) from the inventory items as MT will sell the
inventory items and receive the benefits (i.e. cash flows) upon sale of the
inventory items.
1
MT does not have a present right to payment of the goods. It still needs to sell
the inventory and the proceeds must be used to pay the loan in terms of the loan
terms with XYZ Bank.
1
MT still has legal title of the inventory and has the ability to direct the use of
goods as they continue to trade the inventory. 1
MT has not transferred physical possession of the inventory as it still remains
in the Johannesburg warehouse and XYZ has agreed to have the inventory
remain on the premises.
1
The risks linked to ownership remains with MT such as risk of theft or
damage, price risk (including selling at a discount), risk of non-sale and MT
remains fully obligated for the loan capital and interest repayment irrespective
of the collateral pledged to XYZ Bank irrespective of the sales proceeds generated
from disposal or the inventory.
1
The customers has yet to accept the asset because the inventory items has
not been sold as yet. 1
Consideration should however be given to a possible write-down to net
realisable value, since the inventory will have to be sold at a significant
discount.
1
Financial liability considerations
The loan is a financial liability, being a contractual obligation to deliver cash,
which should be accounted for in terms of IFRS 9 Financial Instruments. 1
IFRS 9 requires that a financial liability be derecognised only when, it is
extinguished, that is, when the obligation specified in the contract is discharged
or cancelled or expires.
1
The extinguishment of the debt will occur when the inventory is sold and the
proceeds are used to pay off the loan and only when the full capital and interest
on the loan is repaid to XYZ Bank. 1
On sale of the inventory, the inventory will be derecognised along with the
corresponding financial liability. 1
There may be a gain or loss on settlement on the difference between the loan
amount and proceeds received which will still accrue to MT as it remains obligated
to repay and settle the capital and accrued interest on the loan to XYZ Bank. 1
Presentation and disclosure considerations
It is incorrect to state that the loan can be offset with the carrying amount of the
inventory as at year end as MT does not have a legally enforceable right to
offset the inventory against the loan balance because the loan remains the
obligation of MT and XYZ has an enforceable right to demand settlement of
the loan.
1

IAS 1 determines that assets and liabilities may not be offset unless another
standard or interpretation permits or requires otherwise such as IAS 32 only
permits financial assets to be offset against financial liabilities and inventory
is not a financial asset.
1
Furthermore, IAS 32.49 determines that it is generally inappropriate to offset
assets that are pledged as collateral against financial liabilities. 1
As none of the pledged inventory was sold, the total balance of inventory it will
be presented as MT financial statement as a current asset. 1
Inventory pledged as security should be disclosed and the R50 million will need
to be disclosed in the inventory note with details of the collateral provided. 1
The loan should be classified as a current liability at year end because the
loan covenants were breached and the loan is therefore repayable on
demand.
1
Conclusion: Sarah is therefore incorrect in her assertion because from the above
one can conclude that the MT has control of the inventory and the substantial
risks and rewards of ownership and therefore it still remains an asset of MT and
should continue to be recognised and not offset against the financial liability
1

Part (f) Describe the steps that AID’s accounting authority should take
following the criticism expressed in the media with regard to the
agreement AID concluded with Inala.
Marks
Before the investigation
Since this is a public entity, inform the relevant minister/ state organ in writing
about:
- the issue at hand;
- the planned approach and scope to deal with this matter; and/or
- Seek confirmation to continue with the plans.
1
Seek legal advice on the matter. 1
Fully cooperate with any external investigations or inquiries launched by relevant
government agencies or regulatory bodies. 1
Inform all other stakeholders about the matter and reassure them of the plans to
address it. 1
The accounting authority could also engage directly with the PDP to explain why
this is a good idea and to obtain their insights and ways forward, in hopes that they
retract the statement.
1
Obtain the assistance of a public relations officer and/or compliance officer to
assist with managing reputational risks. 1
Investigation of the matter
Initiate a comprehensive investigation of the entire matter, as follows: 1
Set the scope of the investigation to be undertaken, in writing, and cover: 1
the entire process leading up to the agreement so that all the facts can be
gathered; and 1
an evaluation of compliance with AID’s internal policies and procedures. 1
Abiding by the appropriate governance over investments policies and procedures,
appoint a competent external firm (to maintain objectivity and credibility) to
undertake the investigation based on the scope in the engagement letter.
1
Press release before the investigation is concluded
Prepare a clear and concise public statement addressing the concerns raised
in the media; the steps being taken to investigate the matter and reaffirm AID's
commitment to transparency and accountability.
1
Based on the outcome of the investigation
Determine the merits of the allegations, i.e. is there a sound rationale for the
investment and are the terms fair. 1
Determine whether there is a need to make improvements to AID’s policies and
procedures to prevent a recurrence of this incident related to conflict of interest and
ethical conduct, and on investment appraisal and decision-making.
1
Consider if the PFMA requirements were not complied with. 1
Take disciplinary action, if any wrongdoing is discovered, against individuals
involved. 1
If it becomes evident that the terms of the agreement were prejudicial to AID, consider
potential legal action against Inala Investments / Jim to recover any losses. 1
If the terms were biased and thus unfair, the accounting authority should consider
backing out of the agreement. 1
Report the findings to the minister/ state organ. 1
Report findings and plan of action to the public (i.e. press release). 1
