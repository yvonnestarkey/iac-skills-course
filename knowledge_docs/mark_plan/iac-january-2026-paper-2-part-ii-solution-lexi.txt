Part (f) Advise MGT on which loan offer it should accept.
• Consider both quantitative and qualitative factors.
• Do not provide calculations.
Marks
Affordability
Before making a funding decision, MGT should consider the following factors:
1 Loan 1 requires one single payment at the end of the five years. Although this gives
MGT some relief during the loan term i.e. more cash efficient, MGT needs to consider
whether it will generate sufficient returns from the investment in CreativeLexi to pay
this when it becomes due or be able to refinance the loan. (cost vs benefit)
The company will also be able to receive tax deductions without a cash outflow
each year which would help to reduce the company’s tax payable.
1
1
2 Loan 2 requires annual repayments of capital plus interest. This will increase the
liquidity requirements to service the loan for MGT on an annual basis. However, the
constant payments will also help reduce the final payment due at the end of the 5-
year loan term.
1
3 MGT must consider the penalties & interest on missed instalments; the risk of
these penalties will make loan 1 more attractive as potential unforeseen cashflow
problems can lead to greater costs. The timing of the costs and their overall liquidity
vs the returns from CreativeLexi. This may strain cash flows.
1
4 For loan 2, the initiation fee is payable/due immediately and annual interest and
capital repayments on the loan. It is unlikely that the investment in CreativeLexi would
have generated sufficient returns to cover such large payments in a short time period.
1
5 A further matter to consider is that the nature of the income from CreativeLexi is not
recurring, and therefore repayment of annual instalments may be more difficult,
potentially causing a mismatch between cash inflows and outflows for obligations.
The bullet repayment profile enables the management of obligations.
1
Cost of debt
6 Loan 1 is a fixed-rate loan while loan 2 is a variable-rate loan (based on prime). If
MGT wants to minimise its exposure to interest rate fluctuations, it should choose
loan 1.
1
7 In addition to the calculated IRR, MGT must consider the risks associated with the
prime interest rates. These might increase by more than anticipated.
1
8 With the recent downward movements of the prime interest rate, there is a scenario
where it may not increase in the near future by the projected amounts, and thus the
cost of loan 2 could be even lower.
1
Loan conditions
9 While loan 1 mandates a single payment at the conclusion of the term, the 30% return
requirement clause is a concern, as non-compliance by CreativeLexi would result in
the loan becoming due immediately.
It should be considered how the company will finance such a payment if this
requirement is not met. This would further strain cash flows.
1
1
10 Furthermore, the 30% return is based on the performance of the investee, which is
not within the control of MGT. Is MGT willing to take such a risk? A sensitivity
analysis on the proposed acquisition will have to be done before committing to such
terms.
In addition, from the scenario, it is not clear how this 30% return will be calculated.
What metrics will be considered / used?
1
1
1
11 With the recent scandals around CreativeLexi and Arno's involvement, CreativeLexi
might actually not provide such returns.
1

Part (g) Based only on the information and data included in the extract from the
report on the social media performance indicators, discuss the factors that
MGT should consider before proceeding with the acquisition of
CreativeLexi.
• Do not provide calculations.
Marks
1 Scope of the report
1.1 Period of assessment: At the point where the report is evaluated, the scope of the
report is limited to two months only, which would be insufficient to determine if the
growth rate is sustainable over time or once-off.
1
1.2 Historical data: The scope only focused on the company, but Lexi has a long track
record of engagements, which could include her personal social media campaigns,
which will not be included in the CreativeLexi projections.
1
1.3 The report indicates that Lexi only referred to five of CreativeLexi’s social media
accounts and there may be more, as well as other personal social media accounts,
which may indicate the data is not complete in assessing growth.
Furthermore, no mention is made of prior marketing campaigns only current ones
were included. This would most likely eliminate successful campaigns that assisted
in Lexi building such a well-known brand for herself and the company.
1
1.4 The report does not consider where CreativeLexi’s social media accounts are no
longer followed, which can also impact growth rates since fewer people may react to
her site.
1
1.5 Additionally, the scope is not disaggregated based on the different marketing
campaigns across CreativeLexi’s social media accounts, which makes it challenging
to determine the consistency or success of the marketing campaigns and how these
campaigns have enhanced social media engagement.
1
1.6 While the data was obtained from social media platforms through authorised access,
the methods used to collect it may be in contravention of the platforms’ policies
and may not be sufficiently accurate, valid, or complete to support MGT's decision-
making.
1
1.7 The report is limited to the social media platforms listed and does not include others,
such as Threads, Pinterest, or BlueSky. However, it should be noted that these
additional platforms are unlikely to be material, as only the major platforms were
provided.
1
1.8 The report lacks additional analysis of follower groups in terms of location, age,
gender, etc., to gain an understanding of the target market and what kind of brands it
would be best for CreativeLexi to target.
1
2 Data analysis
2.1 The report does not explain the process used to clean up the data gathered and data
excluded from the analysis of the results, making it difficult to determine the accuracy
of the findings.
1
2.2 It is not clear if the New Followers quantitative data includes or excludes followers
that may possibly be automated bots on social media platforms. The data has not
been explained well, and cryptic information are provided at times.
1
3 Unrealistic engagement-to-purchase ratios
3.1 There is no analysis of the comments to determine positive, neutral or negative
comments, which would not make it possible to determine the success of marketing
campaigns or how the marketing has impacted users’ perceptions of CreativeLexi
clients. (Reputation is a driver over time for revenue generation.)
1
3.2 Management should consider the fickle nature of social media, and that the
attractiveness, perceptions, and followings can change very quickly and
unexpectedly, and that this risk should be built into the valuation.
1

4.1 The average purchase amount across all the platforms has remained the same,
even though the engagement across the sites varies, raising concerns whether or not
the purchases occurred, or the profiles exist/are an actual person. This is not

1

that engages in questionable actions such as allegedly using bots to create followers,

1
6.4 MGT should consider how closely Lexi’s personal brand is related to CreativeLexi
as a negative impact on one will directly influence the performance of the other.
1
6.5 The absence of benchmarking against industry standards or comparable influencer
conversion rates limits MGT’s ability to assess whether the performance is

how she responded to the allegations in the video.
• Do not discuss the Companies Act and King IV Report on Corporate

Marks
1 General
1.1 Lexi, as the CEO and public face of CreativeLexi (Pty) Ltd, has a legal and ethical
duty to uphold the values of integrity, accountability, and ethical leadership and
her conduct does not uphold these.
1
1.2 Labelling the video as a deepfake rather than seeking expert assistance to prove
it might have been deep faked, undermines her integrity/brings her integrity
into question, and she might not be honest about the events that occurred.
1
2 Ethics triangle
Ethical behaviour must strike a balance between self-preservation and community
responsibility.
Good for self
2.1 Lexi's aggressive defence may have been intended to protect her personal and
professional reputation, particularly as a brand-driven public figure whose business
depends on credibility (good for self).
1
2.2 By indicating that she will share the personal information (data) of the person who
shared the video, Lexi will be in breach of the Protection of Personal Information
Act, and if she is found guilty, she will have to pay a fine to the Information
Regulator or could face jail time (bad for self)
1
2.3 Even if her response helped protect her brand in the short term, the medium and
long-term impact could be negative due to the following:
• Her response undermines the ethical tone of her leadership;
• Her response damages the business’s legitimacy, which could possibly result
in regulatory scrutiny, revenue loss by losing existing clients, loss of potential
clients, and defamation lawsuits.
(bad for self)
1
1
2.4 Threatening to harm an alleged whistleblower would dissuade others from
becoming whistleblowers, and does not serve the interests of others, especially
not her clients, followers, or the public, and she would lose clients and potential
investors who deem such behaviour inappropriate. (bad for self)
1
Good for others
2.5 While it is natural for Lexi to want to defend herself, her aggressive response could
potentially endanger another individual's safety (and perhaps even those close

This creates a hostile online environment, especially given her influence, which
could lead the public to adopt similar behaviours.
(bad for others)
1
1
2.6 Lexi does not appear to consider the adverse consequences of her actions for key

addressing the issue. (bad for others)
1
2.7 Requiring others to harm another person online can be regarded as cyberbullying.
(bad for others) 1

evidenced by her emphasis on individualism and her focus on her own 1
